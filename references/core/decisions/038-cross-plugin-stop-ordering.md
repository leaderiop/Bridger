## Context

[ADR-BR-012](../decisions/012-intent-and-linearization.md) linearizes operations per plugin and rejects one host-wide lock. It leaves one ordering open (URS gap G11). Stopping a provider *P* must first stop each live dependent *D* ([06 — Removal](../behaviors/06-removal.md), BEH-BR-043). That stop of *D* is a *cascade*, and it can meet an operator operation already in flight on *D*: a `restart` of *D* draining its leases, a `replace` of *D*, or a `remove` of *D* parked after `DrainTimeout`. BEH-BR-073 covers the opposite direction only, an operator call arriving while a system stop runs.

The host-core prototype avoided the question by running operator operations one at a time across the whole host. That held up, but it showed the cost ADR-BR-012 predicted, and a worse one. Unrelated operations queued behind any drain. And a lease callback on *D* that called a mutating operation, while another operation waited for *D*'s lease, blocked until that drain timed out. It should have failed fast with `WouldDeadlock`. A per-plugin design has to say:

- whom a cross-plugin stop waits for, and for how long;
- what it does to an operation already in flight on the dependent;
- what happens to a stop that a `DrainTimeout` parked, and who owns it when it resumes;
- why no two host transitions can wait on each other.

A review of the first version of this decision (CCR-BR-008) found that it stated some of these answers too broadly: that every system stop settles by itself, that only a lease callback can close a wait cycle, that a cascade's wait is bounded by the dependent step's own limit followed by its own, and that a whenAvailable block is waited for as a transition step. A re-review of those corrections found further gaps: mount edges drawn from parent to child, which would make a child that uses its parent's services a cycle; a failing cascading call that left the dependency-loss stops it started draining with no owner; every call sharing a stop failed by one call's limit; no limit while a cascade waits on a build; stops taking a `concurrency` slot; and no rule for a drain that expires during shutdown. This revision corrects them.

Terms, as in [08 — Operation legality](../behaviors/08-operation-legality.md): a *transition step* is one build, or one stop, of one plugin, and at most one runs per plugin at a time; a stop step begins when the plugin becomes `stopping` and includes waiting for its dependents' stops, its lease drain and its scope close. The *cascade root* is what began the provider's stop: an operator call (the *cascading call*), a runtime failure, a dependency-loss stop, or shutdown. A stop is *parked* when its drain expired without `force`, or when the failing call that drove it left it unsettled, and it waits for a call, a cascade or shutdown to resume it: a *parked operation* (an operator operation's stop) or a *parked system stop* (a dependency-loss stop). Only stops driven by an operator call — its own stop, or a cascade whose root is an operator call — park.

## Decision

1. **No host-wide operation order.** Calls on one plugin pass steps 1–7 of BEH-BR-188 and reach their linearization points in arrival order, as ADR-BR-012 says. A call of an operation that names a plugin never waits for a call on another plugin to pass those steps or to reach its linearization point. Two waits across plugins exist by design and are outside this rule: `reconcile` serializes its own entries (BEH-BR-156), and `awaitIdle` waits for every transition step running or requested and not yet begun, and for every plugin ready to build (BEH-BR-040). The single audit writer orders *records*, not operations; the writer's queue is the only host-wide point a call passes.
2. **Waits follow dependency edges, from provider to dependent.** A transition step on *P* may wait only for:
   - transition steps of plugins that depend on *P* — that captured a service of *P*, or are children of *P* — directly or transitively;
   - leases on *P* and on those plugins.

   A `whenAvailable` block holding a service of *P* is interrupted and its finalizers are awaited as a lease on *P* (BEH-BR-133); *P*'s stop does not wait for a transition step of the block's plugin and does not stop that plugin on account of the block, though it is still stopped or removed if it is otherwise a dependent or child of *P*. A transition step on *P* never waits for a transition step of a provider of *P*, nor of an unrelated plugin. A build may wait for a free `concurrency` slot; the host never makes it wait for another plugin's transition step, and a wait that the build's author code introduces is bounded by `buildTimeout`. Stops take no slot: `concurrency` bounds builds only, a stop step never waits for any slot, and stops of independent subtrees run concurrently.

   The graph of [INV-BR-002](../invariants.md#inv-br-002-the-dependency-graph-is-acyclic-per-resolution-scope) has dependency edges (dependent→provider) and mount edges (child→parent: a child depends on the parent that mounted it), and is acyclic. A child that requires its parent's services is therefore legal; a parent that requires a service of its own child is a cycle, and a child install that would close one fails with `DependencyCycle` and registers nothing (BEH-BR-121). Every wait between host transitions runs against an edge of that graph — a provider waits for its dependents, a parent for its children — so the wait-for relation is acyclic too, and host transitions cannot deadlock among themselves. A cycle can be closed only by code that runs inside a lease, a build or a finalizer of an activation scope and calls the host. The carried references catch that within the fiber tree with `WouldDeadlock` ([ADR-BR-016](../decisions/016-reentrancy-detection.md)): a lease holder carries its provider in `@bridger/core/HeldPlugins`, and a fiber running *P*'s build, `load` or one of its finalizers carries *P* in `@bridger/core/CurrentTransition`, as does every fiber it forks and awaits before returning. A hand-off to another fiber remains the residual of [RSK-BR-011](../gxp/03-risk-assessment.md#rsk-br-011), bounded by the drain limits and `buildTimeout`.
3. **A cascade waits for the dependent's transition step, never for its operation.** When stopping *P* needs dependent *D* stopped:
   - **No transition step running on *D*, and *D*'s stop not parked:** the host starts a dependency-loss stop of *D*.
   - **A transition step running on *D*** (a build or a stop, whether an operator operation, a system transition or another cascade owns it): the cascade waits for that step to settle. It then stops *D* only if *D* still holds a service of *P*. While it waits — for a stop or for a build — the cascading call's own drain limit runs: on expiry without `force` the call fails with `DrainTimeout` (`id` *D*; `leases` 0 when *D*'s step is a build), every stop it drives that has not settled parks (item 4), and *D*'s step continues under its owner's options; with `force`, *D*'s remaining leases are revoked, and a build, which has none, is waited for under `buildTimeout`. A cascade with no caller waits under `BridgerOptions.drainTimeout` and revokes on expiry; an expiry while it waits for a build continues the wait under `buildTimeout`.
   - **An operator operation in flight on *D* that is not parked:** the cascade never joins, supersedes or cancels it. That operation continues after its step. *P* was hidden in the revision in which its stop began (BEH-BR-042), so no later build of *D* can capture *P*, and *D* lands by availability. Where a running build of *D* is stale both because the operator changed *D*'s target and because *P* is leaving, the operator's operation decides; the result is discarded.
   - ***D* is a child of *P*:** *P*'s stop removes *D* (stop, then unregister) as a dependent, before *P*'s scope begins to close, whatever services *D* holds. The child's system records carry the cascade root's record (BEH-BR-190) as `causationId` — when *P* was stopped directly by an operator call, that call's `requested` record. A parked operation on the child ends (item 5) and the child is removed when its stop completes; if the resumed drain expires again, its stop becomes a parked system stop with `next` "gone".
4. **How a cascade drains depends on its root.**
   - **Operator call at the root:** the dependent's stop drains under the cascading call's `drain` and `force`. On expiry without `force` the call fails with `DrainTimeout { id, realm?, leases, target? }` — `id` and `realm` the plugin whose leases did not drain, or whose running transition step the call was waiting for; `target` the plugin the call named, as `{ id, realm }`, when it differs (CCR-BR-037) — and every stop the call drives that has not settled — its own stop and every dependency-loss stop it started, at any depth — parks in the same revision: the call's own stop as a parked operation, each dependent's as a parked system stop (`reason` "dependency-loss"). Revoking there would apply a `force` the operator did not pass. Only the call under whose options the drain ran fails; `stop-parked.target` names the plugin that call named.
   - **No caller at the root** (a runtime failure, a dependency-loss stop with no operator call at its root, a refused background restart — a refused `whenAvailable` re-run included — shutdown, or a child's removal by its sub-scope close or `Plugin.Runtime.unmount`, for the stops of the child's own dependents, BEH-BR-121): the stop drains under `BridgerOptions.drainTimeout` and revokes on expiry — under shutdown, only once the overall shutdown limit has passed (BEH-BR-107); it never parks. Its system records carry the cascade root's record (BEH-BR-190) as `causationId`: the `runtime-failure` record, shutdown's `requested` record, the child's mount record or `unmount-requested` record, or the cause carried by the root dependency-loss stop's own `system-stop` record.
   - **Once shutdown has begun**, a drain expiry parks no stop, whoever began it: shutdown takes the stop over under its own limits, and a call still waiting on it completes with `HostClosed` (BEH-BR-075).

   A cascading call's total wait is at most its own drain limit for the plugin it named and for each plugin on the dependency path below it, plus `buildTimeout` for each build step it waits for. Finalizer running time is not bounded by the core (BEH-BR-107), so the bound holds for finalizers that return.
5. **A parked stop is resumed by the next call that needs it, and the resumer owns it.** A parked stop never completes by itself (BEH-BR-074). It resumes when:
   - a call joins or supersedes the parked operation (BEH-BR-072): the call resumes it under its own `drain` and `force`, becomes its owner, completes with its outcome, and writes its own `completed` or `failed` record — never `joined` or `superseded` — whose `resumes` is the `recordId` of the parked call's `requested` record. The resumed operation is the one the in-flight table's row names: the operation in flight for a join, the incoming one for a supersede. The parked call, already answered with `DrainTimeout`, writes nothing more;
   - `remove`, `replace`, `restart`, `disable` or `reconfigure` arrives on a plugin whose system stop is parked (BEH-BR-073; on a child whose stop removes it, only `remove`, BEH-BR-125): the call records its intent when it resumes the stop and is the operation in flight from then on (the in-flight table applies to later calls). Its own effects — an override, a `replace`'s reservations — are applied only when it is decided, after the stop settles, by the state table against where the plugin landed; if the stop removed the plugin (a child), `remove` completes. If its own drain expires again, it parks as its own parked operation with nothing of it stored. `retry` and `enable` there fail with `Conflict { pending: "dependency-loss" }`;
   - a cascade from one of the plugin's providers, or from its parent, needs it stopped (BEH-BR-080), under the limits of item 4;
   - shutdown takes it over, under its own limits, never parking again;
   - for a child whose stop was parked outside its parent's stop — by an operator call on the child, or a cascade from another of its providers — the sub-scope it was mounted under closes, or `Plugin.Runtime.unmount` is called for it, before the parent's stop has begun (from the revision in which the parent becomes `stopping`, its activation fails or shutdown begins, whichever comes first) (BEH-BR-121, BEH-BR-126): the removal resumes the stop under `BridgerOptions.drainTimeout`, revokes on expiry and never parks again, shows `reason` "remove" and `next` "gone", emits `stop-resumed` with `by` "sub-scope" or "unmount", and is not a cascade; its records, and the `intent-ended` record of a parked intent it ends, take as `causationId` the child's mount record or the `unmount-requested` record. Any stop of such a child begun outside its parent's stop — an operator call's, another provider's cascade or the child's own system stop — and still draining is taken over, as a cascade with no caller takes over a dependent's running step (item 3): its stop step is neither joined nor cancelled and is the only thing waited for — the drain under `drainTimeout`, revoked at the limit, and an export after it under `handoffTimeout` — never a build; in the revision that step ends the take-over begins the child's removal, so no build of the child begins, and the owning operation and every call waiting on or queued behind the stop are decided against the removal (BEH-BR-073: `remove` completes, any other operation fails with `UnknownPlugin`) — a wait bounded by `drainTimeout`, plus `handoffTimeout` for an export, plus the removal's own bounded steps; a removal a sub-scope close or unmount begins itself on a `starting` child whose build is still queued for a slot abandons that build (CCR-BR-039, CCR-BR-040, CCR-BR-041). A parent's cascade that meets a removal or take-over a sub-scope close or unmount began before the parent's stop waits for it and starts none of its own; only shutdown takes it over (BEH-BR-079; CCR-BR-041). A child stop the parent's stop parked stays the parent's: a sub-scope close or an unmount never resumes it (CCR-BR-037); once the parent is `stopping` or shutdown has begun, the child's removal is the parent's stop's or shutdown's even before it reaches the child, and a sub-scope close or unmount never shortens shutdown's limits (CCR-BR-039, CCR-BR-040). A child whose cleanup fails during any of these removals is recorded `failed` (cleanup) and then unregistered at once, the leak logged as acknowledged by the system (BEH-BR-047); `unmount` then fails with `CleanupError`, and a later mount of the id succeeds; under shutdown it stays registered (CCR-BR-040, CCR-BR-041). A sub-scope finalizer never fails and never waits on a lease the closing fiber carries in `HeldPlugins` (BEH-BR-088), its own or inherited from the fiber that forked it, on a plugin of the child's removal set — the child and its dependents, transitively: with such a lease it does not wait — a removal it would begin or take over goes to the host as a removal with no caller, forked outside the closing fiber, and a removal the parent's stop began stays the parent's — and it logs a warning naming the child; `unmount` from such a fiber, or from one carrying a plugin of that set in `CurrentTransition`, instead fails with `WouldDeadlock { id, realm }` at the call, before any wait and writing no record, whatever removal is under way or would begin (BEH-BR-121, BEH-BR-126; CCR-BR-038, CCR-BR-039).

   **When a cascade or shutdown resumes a parked operator operation:** that operation's intent ends with the resumption. The dependent lands where a dependency-loss stop leads (`waiting`, or `starting` when another provider is available; a child is removed), and the parked operation's remaining actions (unregistering, disabling, swapping the definition, rebuilding) never run. In the revision in which the intent ends, an ended `replace` releases its reservations, every override change made under the intent — by any call — reverts to the override in effect before the first of them, and the host writes an `intent-ended` system record: `endsIntentOf` the `recordId` of the parked call's `requested` record, `configChange.paths` of the reverted paths, and as `causationId` the cascade root's record (BEH-BR-190), or shutdown's `requested` record. The `system-stop` written when the stop settles is an ordinary one. To finish what it asked for, the operator issues the call again, and it is decided from the landed state.

   **When the resumed drain expires again:** only the call under whose options it ran fails with `DrainTimeout`, and every stop that call drives that has not settled parks (item 4). The dependent's stop becomes a parked system stop (`reason` "dependency-loss", `next` the dependency-loss destination — "waiting" or "starting", "gone" for a child — kept current while it stays parked). Any other cascade waiting on that stop finds it parked and resumes it under its own options. From then on item 5's rules for parked system stops apply to it, not the in-flight table.
6. **`WouldDeadlock` is decided before any wait, and again at the intent.** A call's re-entrancy check runs at step 7 of BEH-BR-188 before the call waits for anything a held plugin could block — a transition step on its target, a step on a dependent, a running system stop — and again at the linearization point before its intent is recorded, because only there is it known whether the call joins an operation in flight. A lease holder, build or finalizer that calls an operation on an unrelated plugin is not delayed by any drain. `Plugin.Runtime.unmount` decides its `WouldDeadlock` at the call, before any wait, from the standing condition — the caller holding a lease in the child's removal set, or carrying a plugin of it in `CurrentTransition` — not from whether a removal is under way or has parked (BEH-BR-126; CCR-BR-038, CCR-BR-039).
7. **Parking is visible.** A parked stop shows `parked: true` in its snapshot entry and inspection, the inspection names the operator operation in flight, parking emits `stop-parked` and resumption `stop-resumed` (with who resumed it and the intent it ends, if any), an ended intent has its `intent-ended` record, and a parked stop does not count as a running transition for `awaitIdle`.

## Alternatives considered

- **One host-wide operation lock** (what the prototype did first). Rejected, as in ADR-BR-012: unrelated plugins queue behind a slow drain. The prototype also showed that a lease holder calling any mutating operation blocks until an unrelated drain times out, instead of failing fast or succeeding.
- **The cascade supersedes an operator operation in flight on the dependent.** Rejected: a system stop has no caller to own the outcome. The operator's caller would get `Superseded` for a change it never conflicted with, and the dependent's transition would be redirected mid-way.
- **The cascade waits for the dependent's whole operation, not just its transition step.** Rejected: a dependent's `replace` or `restart` ends with a build, and a parked operation never ends by itself. The provider's removal would then wait on a build it does not need, or forever.
- **The cascade waits for the dependent's step under that step's own limit, then drains under its own** (the first version of this decision). Rejected: the dependent's step may belong to an operation under `drain: "forever"`, and the provider's removal would then wait forever although its own caller set a limit.
- **Every system stop revokes on expiry, so none ever parks.** Rejected: a dependency-loss stop driven by an operator's call would apply `force` the operator did not pass, interrupting callbacks the operator chose not to interrupt (BEH-BR-086). Revocation stays for stops that have no caller to tell.
- **A cascade that resumes a parked operation completes it** (unregisters a parked `remove`, disables a parked `disable`). Rejected: the trail would show a dependent unregistered or disabled under a different operator's call, attributed only through a causation link. Ending the parked intent with the stop keeps each lasting change tied to a call that asked for it.
- **A call that joins a parked operation writes `joined`.** Rejected: the record would link it to a call that writes nothing more, so the joining call's real outcome would be recorded nowhere.
- **Every cascading call that shares a stop fails when its drain expires again.** Rejected: a call whose own limit had not expired would be told `DrainTimeout` for a limit it did not set; it resumes the parked stop under its own options instead.
- **A failing call parks only its own stop.** Rejected: a dependency-loss stop it started would go on draining under the options of a call that has already returned, with nobody to resume or settle it.
- **`retry` and `enable` resume a parked system stop.** Rejected: both only ask to start a plugin; letting them decide how a stuck drain ends would turn a start request into a stop decision.
- **Ordered locks over plugin IDs** (acquire every affected plugin's lock in ID order). Rejected: a cascade's set of dependents is known only as it walks the graph, and holding every lock through a drain recreates the host-wide wait locally.

## Consequences

**Positive**:

- An operation on one plugin never waits for a drain on an unrelated plugin.
- Freedom from deadlock among host transitions follows from an acyclic graph the host already enforces at `install` (and, for children, at child install), rather than from a lock order to maintain.
- A cascade cannot overrule a running operator operation, and a parked intent is never completed under another operator's call.
- Every resumed stop has exactly one owner, and every call exactly one terminal record.
- A re-entrant call from a lease callback, a build or a finalizer fails at once in every case the carried reference can see.

**Negative**:

- A cascading call's total wait is bounded by its own drain limit once per plugin on the dependency path, plus `buildTimeout` for each build step it waits for — a bound that grows with the depth of the graph below the plugin it names, and that assumes finalizers that return.
- An operator who parked a `remove` of a dependent, and whose dependent was then stopped by a provider's removal, finds the dependent `waiting`, not gone, and must call `remove` again. The trail shows why (the `intent-ended` record).
- A dependency-loss stop under an operator's cascade can park; until an operator resumes it, `retry` and `enable` on that plugin are refused with `Conflict { pending: "dependency-loss" }`.

**Trade-off accepted**: a cascade bounded by its own drain limit per plugin on its path, parked system stops that an operator must resume, and parked intents that a provider's removal ends rather than completes, in exchange for no host-wide order, no deadlock among transitions, no `force` the operator did not pass, and no lasting change made under the wrong call.
