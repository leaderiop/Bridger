Bridger improvement report 07

Specification quality, traceability, formal guarantees and verification

Bridger's spec is far more traceable than Cordis's paper (BEH/INV/REQ/RSK/ADR/CCR ids, 14 mechanical checks, an in-spec test-host contract) but far less executable. 52 invariants are prose; the prototype cited 2 distinct INV ids against 223 BEH ids when this was written (it now names 40 of 187 invariants and 395 of 1,054 behaviours). Nine audit rounds keep finding the same four classes, always in the previous round's fix (round 8: 4 H / 24 M / 36 L; round 9: 6 H / 23 M / 27 L), and the mechanical verifiers pass every time, so they police form, not meaning. The four highest-value changes are one executable reference model, a spec-to-code correspondence table, a gate-walk process rule, and fast-check model-based suites.

Not checked / uncertainties

  • Properties are checked on sampled histories (60 to 300 per run with shrinking), not proved. The model covers the settled behaviour of the dependency graph, drain bounds, wait-for cycles and the audit predicates; leases beyond the drain bound, handoff, hooks, lazy registrations, federation and the companion trees are checked only by the scenario suites.
  • R8 blocks for the 14 companion trees are formulaic (scope is the invariant's title, predicate `none`), so they pass the linter without being a considered assessment; only the core blocks were authored by reading each invariant.
  • The correspondence table counts a mention of an id in src or test, so a citation says where to look, not that the rule holds; tests may cover a behaviour without naming it.
  • No CI runner is configured; verify-spec.sh is the target to wire into one. The audit counts (round 8: 4/24/36, round 9: 6/23/27) are from the report summaries, and whether Highs fall in future rounds is not yet known.
  • Cost estimates in the original review were rough.
